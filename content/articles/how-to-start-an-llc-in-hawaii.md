# How to Start an LLC in Hawaiʻi

Forming a limited liability company (LLC) is one of the most common ways to structure a small business in Hawaiʻi. It separates your personal assets from business liabilities while keeping the paperwork relatively simple. Here's a step-by-step walkthrough of what's actually required.

## Step 1: Choose and Check Your LLC Name

Your company name must contain "Limited Liability Company," "L.L.C.," or "LLC." "Limited" may be abbreviated as "Ltd." and "Company" may be abbreviated as "Co."

The name also must not be substantially identical to a name already on file with the Department of Commerce and Consumer Affairs (DCCA). Before you commit to a name or start branding, search the state's business name database through Hawaiʻi Business Express to confirm it's available. If you're not ready to file yet but want to lock in a name, you can file a name reservation, which holds the name for 120 days.

## Step 2: Appoint a Hawaiʻi Registered Agent

Every Hawaiʻi LLC must designate a registered agent — an individual or entity authorized to accept legal documents (service of process) on the company's behalf. An individual registered agent must reside in Hawaiʻi, and the registered agent must maintain a business address in Hawaiʻi. This can be:

- A member or manager of the LLC who resides in Hawaiʻi
- Another individual who resides in Hawaiʻi
- A commercial registered agent service

## Step 3: File Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1)

This is the document that legally creates your LLC. It's filed with the DCCA's Business Registration Division (BREG) — Hawaiʻi does not have a separate Secretary of State office; BREG handles this function.

Form LLC-1 asks for:

- The LLC's name
- The mailing address of the initial principal office
- The registered agent's name and address
- Whether the LLC will be member-managed or manager-managed
- If manager-managed, the name and address of each initial manager and the number of initial members
- If member-managed, the name and address of each initial member
- Whether the duration is "at-will" (ongoing/perpetual) or for a specified term
- Whether members will be personally liable for company debts (this is optional and rarely elected)

**Filing fee:** $50, plus a mandatory $1 State Archives fee ($51 total). Fees are non-refundable. The fee schedule lists expedited review at an additional $25; however, BREG currently states that expedited handling is unavailable during its 2026 system transition. Confirm current availability before filing.

**How to file:**
- **Online** through Hawaiʻi Business Express — generally the most efficient filing method
- **By mail**, email, or fax to BREG, which generally takes longer to process
- **In person** at the BREG office: 335 Merchant Street, Room 201, Honolulu, HI 96813

Once approved, you'll receive confirmation along with your assigned file number, and your LLC becomes searchable in the state's business registry.

## Step 4: Draft an Operating Agreement

Hawaiʻi does not require an LLC to file an operating agreement with the state, but it's strongly recommended. This internal document governs how the company is run — ownership percentages, how profits and losses are allocated, management structure, voting rights, and what happens if a member leaves. Without one, disputes between members default to whatever Hawaiʻi's LLC statute provides, which may not reflect what the members actually intended.

## Step 5: Get an EIN from the IRS

Many LLCs need a federal Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). A multi-member LLC generally needs one, as does an LLC with employees or certain federal tax obligations. A single-member LLC with no employees and no applicable excise-tax obligation may not need a separate EIN for federal tax purposes, although it may choose or need one for banking or other purposes. Applying is free directly through the IRS.

## Step 6: Register for Hawaiʻi's General Excise Tax (GET)

Hawaiʻi doesn't have a traditional sales tax — instead, virtually all business activity is subject to the General Excise Tax. Before doing business, you'll need to register with the Hawaiʻi Department of Taxation:

1. Complete Form BB-1 (State of Hawaiʻi Basic Business Application)
2. File online through Hawaiʻi Tax Online, by mail, or in person
3. Pay the one-time $20 registration fee

Once registered, you'll be responsible for filing periodic GET returns (Form G-45) and an annual reconciliation (Form G-49) going forward.

## Step 7: Look Into Other Licenses and Permits

Depending on your industry and location, you may need additional professional or business licenses at the state or county level — for example, contractor's licenses, health permits, or liquor licenses. Check with the relevant state licensing board and your county for anything specific to your line of business.

## Step 8: Keep Up With Your Annual Report

Once your LLC is formed, Hawaiʻi requires an Annual Report each year to keep it in good standing. The due date isn't a single statewide date — it's tied to the quarter in which your LLC was originally registered:

| Formed Between | Annual Report Due |
|---|---|
| January 1 – March 31 | March 31 |
| April 1 – June 30 | June 30 |
| July 1 – September 30 | September 30 |
| October 1 – December 31 | December 31 |

Your LLC is exempt from filing in the same calendar year it was formed — the first report is due the year after formation. The standard LLC annual-report filing fee is $15; DCCA currently charges $12.50 for online filing. The report can be submitted through Hawaiʻi Business Express.

## What It Costs to Get Started

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Articles of Organization (Form LLC-1) | $50 + $1 archives fee = $51 |
| Expedited review | +$25 when available; currently unavailable during BREG's 2026 system transition |
| GET license registration (Form BB-1) | $20 |
| Annual Report (ongoing, yearly) | $15 standard fee; currently $12.50 online |

## A Few Things to Watch For

- **Hawaiʻi has no franchise tax on LLCs**, but the General Excise Tax applies to gross receipts rather than profit, so it's owed even in years with thin margins.
- **Filing fees are non-refundable**, so double-check your Articles of Organization for accuracy before submitting.
- **Missing your Annual Report** can eventually put your LLC out of good standing and, after continued delinquency, lead to administrative dissolution.

## Where to Get Help

For questions about Articles of Organization or registered agent requirements, contact the DCCA Business Registration Division at (808) 586-2727. For GET registration and tax filing questions, contact the Hawaiʻi Department of Taxation. Both agencies' current forms and fee schedules are the best place to confirm exact figures before you file.
